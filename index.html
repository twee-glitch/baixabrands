Home · The Fabric

The Right Layer,
every season.

Layering isn't fashion. It's how warmth, breathability, and safety meet, on a body too small to regulate its own temperature.

We design every TotnKids garment to layer. A swaddle isn't a swaddle on its own. It sits over a bodysuit, in a room with a temperature, on a night with its own weather. Every piece is tested with every other piece.

"Layering is the quietest kind of design. You only feel it when it's right."

Our base layer is bamboo viscose, soft, breathable, gentle on skin that's still becoming itself. The outer layers are organic cotton, insulated to a measured TOG rating, tested to BS EN 16781:2019, the current British Standard for infant sleep garments. Nothing is approximate.

Why bamboo viscose?

Bamboo viscose is breathable, hypoallergenic, and softer than cotton, particularlyimportant for skin that's still developing. It regulates temperature better than synthetics, holds dye in OEKO-TEX Class I non-toxic processes, and gets softer with every wash rather than rougher. Cotton stiffens. Bamboo settles in.

Made by Thygesen, Vietnam

Thygesen Textile in Vietnam is one of the few facilities that combines fair-wage practice with the technical precision needed for medical-grade infant fabric standards. They've been making bamboo viscose for decades; we visit twice a year.

Home · The Science

The science of safer sleep,
in plain language.

TOG, temperature, and why the maths matters more than the marketing. Everything we know about how babies sleep, andwhy we made TotnKids the way we did.

"What should my baby wear to sleep?" is the most common question new parents ask. The answer involves three things: room temperature, what baby is wearing underneath, and what's on top. Get the maths right and your baby is comfortable. Get it wrong and they're either too warm (the biggest risk factor in safer sleep guidance) or unsettled.

TOG, Thermal Overall Grade, is the unit that measures how much insulation a sleep garment provides. It's the same unit duvets use. Higher TOG = warmer. A 3.5 TOG sleep bag is roughly equivalent to a winter duvet for an adult. A 0.2 TOG is summer-light. Fewer, better layers, every season.

"Don't dress for the weather outside. Dress for the room inside."

The standard we test to

BS EN 16781:2019 is the current British Standard for children's sleep bags. It specifies how the TOG rating is measured, what fabric weights count, how the garment must fit, and what safety features it must have. Every TotnKids sleep garment is independently tested to this standard. Our certificates are on the homepage.

Why temperature, not weather

British weather is unhelpfully variable. Your nursery temperature is much more stable than what's happening outside. A small thermometer in the nursery (£8 from any pharmacy) plus our tool removes all the guesswork.

Informed by, not endorsed by

We follow the established guidance from the Lullaby Trust, the NHS, and UNICEF UK Baby Friendly. We don't claim endorsement, weencourage every parent to read their full guidance directly. Links to their official guidance are in our Safer Sleep section.

Home · The First Night

For the
very first night.

Bringing your baby home is a moment. This is what we wish we'd packed and known.

You'll be tired. The hospital bag will be heavier than you remember packing. The car seat will take longer to fit than expected. And then, eventually, you'll bring your tiny person through your front door for the first time.

We can't help with most of that. But we can help with what your baby wears the first time they sleep in their own cot, and what's nearby to make that easier.

LIFESTYLEHospital bag with TotnKids swaddle visible on top.
STUDIOFirst Nights Kit unboxed, swaddle, bodysuits, welcome card.

The first-night essentials

One bamboo swaddle in 1.0 TOG (the most common UK nursery range). Two long-sleeve bodysuits underneath, oneto wear, one to change into. A handwritten safer-sleep cheat sheet. That's it. Anything else is bonus.

Where to put baby down

On their back, in a clear cot, in your room for at least the first six months (per Lullaby Trust guidance). No loose bedding, no pillows, no soft toys. A fitted sheet and a sleep garment is everything.

The room

16–20°C is ideal. Anywhere above 24°C and you'll want to drop the TOG of what baby is wearing. A small nursery thermometer is the most useful £8 you'll spend.

"You'll know more by morning than you do tonight. That's how this works."

Home · Sizing Guide

Sizing,
weight-led.

Babies don't grow in months, theygrow in weight. Pick by your baby's current weight, not the label age.

If you take one thing from this page: sizing is weight-led, not age-led. A 3-month-old in the 90th centile can be wearing what some brands call "6 months." Don't fight that, usethe weight. Our sizing is consistent across swaddles, sleep bags, bodysuits and daywear.

How a TotnKids swaddle should fit

Snug at the chest and shoulders (so baby can't slip down into it), with room at the legs and hips, hip-healthyIHDI-approved cut. The fabric should feel taut on the torso but the legs should be free to move in a natural frog-leg shape.

When to size up

When two of these are true: the cuffs of the bodysuit ride above the wrist, the swaddle feels tight across baby's chest when zipped, or you can no longer do up the bottom snaps. Don't wait for all three, bamboosoftens but doesn't stretch.

Home · Care & Washing

Care for them,
they'll last.

Bamboo viscose gets softer with every wash, ifyou treat it kindly. Here's how.

Machine washing

Wash at 30°C with a gentle detergent. Skip the fabric softener, itcoats the fibres and reduces breathability. Wash with similar colours; the natural dyes are stable but the first wash may release a tiny amount of pigment.

Drying

Low-tumble or air-dry. Bamboo dries faster than cotton, so a short tumble is usually enough. Avoid direct sunlight on dyed pieces, itfades the soft tones over time.

Storage

Fold rather than hang to preserve shape. Store in a cool, dry place. Avoid plastic bags long-term, bambooprefers to breathe.

Stain treatment

Treat stains within 24 hours with cold water and a small amount of mild detergent. Avoid bleach. For stubborn stains, a 30-minute soak in cool water with a gentle pre-wash is usually enough.

What not to do

  • Don't wash hotter than 40°C, itweakens the fibres.
  • Don't bleach, itstrips colour and weakens fabric.
  • Don't iron the printed/embroidered details directly.
  • Don't dry-clean, the chemicals are too harsh for bamboo.

"Wash kindly. They'll last through more babies than you'd think."
